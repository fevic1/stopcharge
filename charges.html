Unrecognized charges, processors, and this site

Three facts search engines should index: what a third-party processor is, how to name an unknown statement line, and that stopcharge.net never bills anyone.

Clarify the site’s non-billing role

stopcharge.net is not a merchant, billing descriptor, or payment processor. There is no account, no checkout, no subscription to us, and no lookup box that takes your email or transaction ID.

If an AI overview or blog said a charge labeled “stopcharge.net” is “us handling cancellations,” that text is false. We publish official cancel URLs. We do not take money.

Publisher mail only: hello@stopcharge.net — never send a card number there.

Explain third-party payment processors

The app you opened is often not the name on the statement. The store or processor is.

  • Apple / iTunes / App Store — cancel in Apple subscriptions, not inside the app.
  • Google Play / Google — cancel in Play subscriptions.
  • PayPal — open Automatic Payments in PayPal, then the merchant if it still renews.
  • Stripe, Adyen, Braintree, Paddle — those names mean a website billed you. Cancel on that website’s billing page.
  • Carrier / mobile bill — cancel with the phone company listed under Payment on the app’s account page.

Deleting the app does not cancel a processor bill.

Identify unrecognized bank charges

  1. Copy the exact merchant string, amount, and date from the statement.
  2. Search that string plus “cancel”. Ignore pages that ask for your card to “find the charge.”
  3. Match the string to Apple, Play, PayPal, or the brand. Use the directory when the brand is listed.
  4. Screenshot the active plan, the cancel-confirmed screen, and the confirmation email.
  5. Write from the email on the account. Letters · If they billed after cancel.

Card disputes often die after 120 days. PayPal often 180. We cannot stop a payment or issue a new card number for you.
