StopCharge

They charged you anyway

This page is for money already taken. It is not a shortcut around the official cancel. Banks ask “did you cancel with the merchant first?” Have that answer.

Order of operations

  1. Cancel on the official path. Keep the confirmation.
  2. Save the evidence below before you close the tab.
  3. Send the refund demand. Give them a date.
  4. If they ignore you or bill again, dispute the charge with the card issuer or PayPal.
  5. If they still refuse, file with a regulator. That is pressure, not a courtroom.

Evidence checklist

  • Screenshot the active subscription page before you cancel (price, renewal date, plan name).
  • Screenshot the final Cancellation confirmed screen with date and time visible.
  • Save the confirmation email as a file. Search “cancelled” / “canceled” / “receipt”.
  • Note chat or call: date, time, agent name or ID, what they promised.
  • Keep the bank / card statement line that shows the charge.
  • If they hid the button: screenshot the maze (how many screens, no cancel in the menu).

A chargeback without this packet is a story. A packet is a file.

Dispute windows (typical, not a promise)

  • Visa / Mastercard / Amex / Discover: often about 120 days from the charge (or from when the service should have stopped) for “cancelled recurring still billed.” Your issuer can be stricter. Use the dispute flow in the bank app first.
  • US billing-error letter (credit cards): written notice generally within 60 days of the statement that first showed the error. Address is on the statement, not always the payment address.
  • PayPal: open a case in the Resolution Center. Item-not-received style claims: up to 180 days from payment. Escalate to a claim within 20 days of opening the dispute or it can auto-close.

Valid reasons that match this site: cancelled, still billed; recurring charge you did not authorize after cancel; duplicate charge. “I used the service and changed my mind” is a weak dispute.

How to file the card dispute

  1. Open the bank or card app → the charge → Dispute / Report a problem.
  2. Pick the recurring / cancelled-subscription reason if it exists.
  3. Attach the screenshots and the demand email.
  4. Write three sentences: what you bought, when you cancelled, that they billed after that date.
  5. Ask for a written case number.

Stop the next one

After cancel: freeze or replace the card they have on file.

  • US: Privacy.com merchant-locked cards.
  • UK / EEA / many other Revolut markets: Revolut virtual card; disposable card for one-off checkouts (not for a subscription you intend to keep).
  • Wise: virtual cards where Wise offers them in your country.
  • Everywhere: the bank app’s “virtual card” or “lock this merchant.” If none exists, cancel the plastic and update only the merchants you still want.

Escalate outside the company

  • US: FTC ReportFraud, CFPB complaint, BBB.
  • UK: Citizens Advice consumer, Financial Ombudsman for the bank side.
  • EU / EEA: European consumer redress / ECC-Net.
  • Thailand: Office of the Consumer Protection Board (OCPB) complaint channels on ocpb.go.th.

Naming a regulator in a polite refund email sometimes moves the ticket. It is not a threat letter. Keep the tone flat.

Warn the next person

If cancel required a phone tree, five extra screens, or they billed after confirmation: open a dark-pattern issue. Screenshots only. No account numbers.
